Smart Home Automation Tips

Automation saves energy when it removes the need to remember. The best automations are simple rules tied to prices, solar output, occupancy and time of day.

Rules worth setting up
- Delay-start large appliances: dishwashers, washing machines and dryers can start automatically at the beginning of the off-peak period or when the solar forecast is strong.
- Schedule EV charging in the car or charger app rather than plugging in and charging immediately when you arrive home in the evening, which is usually the most expensive time.
- Use occupancy and geofencing: let the thermostat set back when the last person leaves and recover shortly before someone returns.
- Turn off standby loads with smart plugs or switched power strips on entertainment centres and home offices overnight.

Price-aware automation
- Many utilities publish time-of-use schedules. A home energy app or smart hub can read the schedule and shift flexible loads automatically.
- Group loads into three kinds: shiftable (dishwasher, laundry, EV, pool pump, water heater), reducible (HVAC setpoint, lighting) and fixed (refrigerator, medical equipment). Automate the first two; never the third.

Solar-aware automation
- Rules such as "start the dishwasher when solar generation exceeds household use by 1 kW" use solar power that would otherwise be exported for little value.
- Combine a forecast with a deadline: "finish charging the car by 7:00, using the sunniest hours first and off-peak grid power for the rest."

Good practice
- Keep a manual override for every automation, and review the schedules seasonally, because sunrise, sunset and prices change.
- Check that devices report their energy use; you cannot improve what you cannot measure.
- Keep smart-home devices updated and on a separate network where your router allows it; they are computers on your network.
