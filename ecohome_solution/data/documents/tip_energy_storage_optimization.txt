Energy Storage Optimization: Home Batteries and Load Shifting

A home battery stores energy when it is cheap or plentiful and releases it when it is expensive. Even without a battery, some devices store energy in other forms.

Battery strategies
- Solar self-consumption: charge the battery from surplus midday solar and discharge in the evening, instead of exporting the surplus and buying back later.
- Time-of-use arbitrage: charge from the grid during off-peak hours and discharge during peak hours. This saves money only when the price gap exceeds the energy lost in a full cycle; round-trip efficiency is often around 85 to 90 percent.
- Keep a backup reserve, for example 20 percent, if you rely on the battery during outages.
- Avoid unnecessary full charge and discharge cycles; most batteries last longer when kept away from the extremes for long periods.

Storage you may already have
- Thermal storage: a well-insulated home stores heat and coolness, which is why pre-cooling and pre-heating work.
- Water heaters store energy as hot water. Heating water during off-peak or solar hours and coasting through the peak shifts that load without any loss of comfort.
- An electric vehicle is a large battery on wheels. Smart charging, and where supported vehicle-to-home, can shift many kilowatt-hours.

Working out whether storage pays
- Saving per day is roughly the energy shifted multiplied by the price difference, minus losses.
- Compare the annual saving with the installed cost to estimate a simple payback period, and remember that batteries lose some capacity over the years.
